SLIDE 01 · THE LOGLINE
India’s Supreme Court promised control.
But what ensued was chaos.

Driven by the fear of rabies, dogs' killings have spiked nationwide in just eight months. Man’s best friend is now the enemy number one.

A grieving filmmaker picks up his camera to investigate what’s going wrong.
SLIDE 02 · WHY THIS FILM, WHY NOW

Why now.

THE LAW THAT MADE SENSE

In 2023, the Indian government passed the Animal Birth Control Rules. Catch. Sterilize. Vaccinate. Return. At scale. Humane and scientifically sound. The onus fell on the overworked municipal bodies.

THE RULING THAT BACKFIRED

After a 6-year-old girl in Delhi died of rabies in June 2025, the Supreme Court ruled on the “street dog menace.” It ordered stray dogs relocated into overcrowded municipal pounds.

POUNDS ARE BLOODBATHS

Dogs are territorial. A hundred strangers, one cage. In eight months, dog killings spiked out of control nationwide.

MISINFORMATION BURNS

Fear spread like wildfire. Mobilized by the housing. Families who fed the dogs for years suddenly found neighbours at the gate.

IN TELANGANA ALONE, OVER 2,800 DOGS WERE ILLEGALLY POISONED AND DUMPED IN OPEN PITS.

A collision between two legitimate truths. Nobody has figured out how to reconcile them.

SLIDE 04 · THE JOURNEY BEGINS
December 5th, 2025.
My pug Brando , Mr.B , breathed his last in the arms of my family. Kidney failure. He was 10.

I picked up my camera because I had to put the grief somewhere. I went looking for the first person who had also loved Mr.B. That person was Cynthia Moses. Talking to her, I understood the film I was making was too small.

This could not just be a letter to Brando. It had to be a love letter to every dog on every street.
MR. B , my pug. The grief that became a camera, and then a love letter to every dog on every street.

SLIDE 06 · THE OTHER SIDE OF THE STREET

No hero. No villain.

The public safety side.

Their argument is nothing but facts. A child died of rabies in Delhi. In densely packed Indian neighbourhoods, children and the elderly live with a real threat. Countries have eliminated rabies within their borders; India has not. As long as the disease exists, they argue, dogs have no place in the middle of modern society.

Fear is real. Misinformation burned.

Rabies kills. Deaths by bite are rare, but not zero. It is fear that drives some people to reach for the poison , and when it spreads unchecked, neighbourhood after neighbourhood turns against the very dogs a feeder has spent years caring for.

Animal activists vs. public safety. Neither side is wrong.

ANIMAL RIGHTS ACTIVISTS , campaigning for the elimination of rabies in Chennai.
SLIDE 07 · THE CRISIS
2,800
Dogs illegally poisoned, dumped in open pits

The Supreme Court promised control. What followed was chaos.

My own family felt it. Our own street dogs, neutered, vaccinated, never aggressive , suddenly the whole neighbourhood demanded they be taken to the pounds.

I saw, first hand, how misinformation bakes into real fear.

SLIDE 08 · THE VACCINATION GAP

The cure exists. The will doesn’t.

0
Herd-immunity threshold (WHO)
0
Vaccination coverage achieved
0
Of disease burden spent on dog vaccines

India never gets close to the 70% target at a national level. A national catch-vaccinate-release drive would require an estimated 1.1 million vaccinators , infrastructure that does not exist. Investment in dog vaccination is under 0.5% of the disease’s economic burden. India carries about 36% of all global rabies deaths.

The proof it can work: Goa hit 70% coverage. Zero confirmed human rabies from 2018–2023.

SLIDE 10 · ARTISTIC VISION

CINEMA VERITE.

No tripods. No formal setups.

The camera moves the way the dogs move.

It observes. It follows. It lingers.

A constant invisible presence. I get things wrong on camera. I change my mind. No talking heads, only conversations. Retro Tamil music and the warmth of a flamenco guitar carry the chaos and tenderness of Chennai’s streets. The film ends where it began: quiet, personal. And a walk through a Decathlon with Brando.

SLIDE 11 · THE FILMMAKER
Who am I, and why me?

I fell in love with cinema when my dad put on a VCD of Titanic when I was 10. I studied cinematography at the Madras Film Institute. Eight years across Tamil, Telugu and Hindi feature films, in the cinematography crews of PC Sreeram, Yamini Yagnamurthy, Sivakumar Vijayan and Sethu Sriram , from assistant to camera operator.

I dismissed documentaries as a lesser art. Then COVID struck. Baraka, Samsara, Chronos and Koyaanisqatsi, back to back, with my editor and best friend Aswin. Reality is indeed stranger than fiction. The seed was planted.

I did not go looking for this story. A seed planted years ago finally bore fruit. It was time I picked up my camera.

Where it lands

Community screenings in the places where dog or man has been harmed by the other. Nothing coast. No easy answers.

SLIDE 16 · INTENDED IMPACT

Eliminate two diseases.

RABIES & FEAR

India needs to up its vaccination and sterilization drive. Once rabies is gone, the epidemic of fear will gradually ease.

The fight can be won by a power Mr.B had.

The power of love.

Cinema cannot cause change. But I believe cinema can initiate discussion. And that’s the first step to change.
